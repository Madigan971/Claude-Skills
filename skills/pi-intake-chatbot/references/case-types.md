## Motor vehicle accident
Most common PI matter. Key facts:
- Vehicles/roles: who was driving, who was passenger, cars vs. truck (commercial trucks change everything — carrier, DOT, logs), motorcycle, bicycle, pedestrian, rideshare (Uber/Lyft — was app on?).
- Crash mechanics: date, time, location (state/road), how it happened, direction/impact point, approximate speed, airbags deployed, vehicle drivable/towed/totaled.
- Authorities: police called? report number? was anyone cited or arrested (esp. DUI)?
- Injuries & treatment: transported by ambulance? ER? still treating?
- Other driver: identified? insured? commercial vehicle or on-the-job driver?
- Their own coverage: liability, UM/UIM, MedPay/PIP (matters a lot in no-fault states).
- Any recorded statement given to an insurer yet?

## Slip / trip and fall & premises liability
Causation and notice are everything:
- Where exactly (business name + address; store aisle, sidewalk, stairwell, parking lot, private residence).
- What caused the fall (wet floor, spill, ice, uneven surface, broken stair, poor lighting, obstruction).
- How long the hazard was there / whether staff knew or should have known; any warning signs present.
- Was an incident report made with the property/business? Photos of the hazard? Witnesses?
- Footwear/activity (they'll be asked about comparative fault — gather neutrally).
- Injuries, ER/doctor, ongoing treatment.
- Property type: business, landlord/apartment, government property (park, sidewalk, public building → notice deadlines, see screening).

## Medical malpractice
High-cost, high-scrutiny cases with **shorter deadlines** and often a pre-suit affidavit/expert requirement. Gather thoroughly and flag for attorney:
- Provider(s) and facility involved; type of care (surgery, diagnosis/missed diagnosis, medication, birth injury, ER).
- What happened and what they believe went wrong; when it occurred and **when they first learned** something was wrong (discovery matters).
- Resulting harm/additional treatment/corrective surgery.
- Whether they've obtained medical records.
- Note: outcome alone ≠ malpractice; do not opine — this is exactly an attorney-with-expert determination.

## Product liability / defective product
- Product name, manufacturer, model, purchase date/source; **still have the product?** (preservation is critical — tell them not to discard or repair it).
- How it failed and how it caused injury; was it used as intended?
- Any recall known? Warnings/instructions present?
- Injuries and treatment; keep receipts/packaging.

## Dog bite / animal attack
- Animal type; owner known? (name/address) — a claim usually needs an identifiable owner/keeper.
- Where it happened; was the victim lawfully present; provoked or unprovoked.
- Prior known aggression/bites (matters in some states); animal control report filed? rabies/vaccination status.
- Injuries, ER, stitches, scarring, infection, ongoing treatment (children and facial injuries are significant — handle with extra care).

## Wrongful death
Lead with condolences; go slowly. See compliance.md (vulnerable callers).
- Decedent's name, date and cause of death, and the underlying incident (crash, malpractice, product, etc. — apply that section too).
- Relationship of the caller to the decedent; surviving spouse/children/parents; whether an estate has been opened / personal representative named (flag standing for attorney — don't adjudicate).
- Financial dependents and impact.

## Nursing home / elder abuse & neglect
- Facility name/location; resident's name and relationship to caller; is caller the authorized representative/POA?
- Nature of harm (falls, bedsores/pressure ulcers, malnutrition/dehydration, medication errors, physical/financial abuse, wandering/elopement).
- When noticed; whether reported to the facility or the state/ombudsman; photos, records.
- Current safety of the resident (if ongoing danger, urge protective steps/reporting).

## Workplace injury (and the workers' comp boundary)
- Injured **on the job**? If the only responsible party is the employer, this is usually **workers' compensation**, a separate system — flag it; the firm may or may not handle comp.
- Screen for a **third-party** PI claim alongside comp: injured by a non-employer (another driver while working, a defective machine/product, a subcontractor, a property owner). Third-party liability keeps it in PI territory.
- Gather employer, date, how it happened, what was reported, and any third parties involved.

## Not our lane (route elsewhere)
Politely flag/decline and suggest the right kind of help; don't attempt these as PI:
- Pure workers' comp with no third party (unless the firm handles comp).
- Criminal matters, family law, immigration, employment discrimination, contract disputes, property/landlord (non-injury), Social Security disability, pure insurance bad-faith without injury.
- Incidents with no injury and no damages.
When declining for scope, be kind and, if the firm keeps a referral list, offer it.
